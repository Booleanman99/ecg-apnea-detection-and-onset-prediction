# ANALYSIS — Is detection leaking, or is short-horizon prediction hard?

**Short answer.** Detection is not leaking. With labels shuffled across the whole training set the feature model falls to the prevalence line, a label-shift test places the information at the labelled minute, and a regression test proves that no Task B input reads a sample at or after the prediction time. Prediction is hard in a specific and now-measured way: most of the one-minute-ahead skill lives in the last 60 seconds before the prediction time. Cut the history off one minute earlier and within-night ranking falls close to chance (on the three validation nights where it is defined). At minute-label resolution we cannot separate an onset already under way, which the grid still labels N, from a short precursor in that final minute; two observations lean toward the former. Either way the model offers little lead time beyond one minute. The pre-registered bar for "learnable" is met on its literal reading and not on the stricter one, and it tests above-chance within-night ranking at a one-minute horizon, nothing more.

**Scope note.** The controls below were run on the round-1 pipelines, which is where the leakage-versus-difficulty question has to be answered: they are the models whose Task B number the pre-registered bar was written for. The round-2 finals (REPORT §4–6: a five-minute-context detector and a logistic-regression onset model, selected by 22-night cross-validation under the rule in `PREREGISTRATION.md`) change the detection numbers substantially and the prediction numbers within noise. They do not change any conclusion here, since the gap sweep, horizon sweep and shuffles concern the information in the signal, not a particular model.

All numbers below are from `outputs/controls.md` (s8, test split), `outputs/posthoc_controls.md` (s9, validation split only), `outputs/prereg_bar.md` (s10), `outputs/error_analysis.md` and `outputs/error_analysis_round2.md` (s7) and `outputs/posthoc_gap_sweep_lr.md` (s9), as mean ± sd over 3 seeds where a model is stochastic. XGB-rel denotes XGBoost with night-so-far features. Items marked **post-hoc** were decided after the test numbers were first seen (29 Oct 2026, 12:08 IST); those that train new models were computed on the validation split; those that re-analyse the fixed test predictions are labelled as such. The stamped texts and their timestamps are in `PREREGISTRATION.md`.

## 1. Ruling out a leaking detection setup

- **Alignment, verified not assumed.** Every annotation in every record sits at sample k·6000 from sample 0, in steps of exactly 6000 (`outputs/alignment.json`; s1 asserts it).
- **Label-shift test (post-hoc, validation).** We re-scored validation predictions against labels shifted by k minutes within each night. For the feature models the profile is centred between 0 and +1. XGB-rel AUROC is 0.743 at k = 0 and 0.745 at +1, falling 0.009 at −1, 0.018 at +2 and to 0.705 / 0.716 at ±3 (seed 0; all seeds within 0.008); logistic regression 0.778 / 0.784, then 0.770 at −1 and 0.754 / 0.758 at ±3. A one-minute misalignment would centre the profile on ±1 or ±2, and the sample-grid check rules that out independently. Every feature-model seed tilts to +1 by 0.002–0.006, so minute-k features carry a little of minute k+1's label. That is the same one-minute lead Task B exploits (§3), and one of the two observations that lean toward the "onset already under way" reading there. The CNN is flat across k (0.871–0.878 for seed 0; 0.892–0.896 and 0.878–0.887 for seeds 1 and 2). Its output barely depends on which minute's label it is scored against, the first sign that it carries night-level rather than minute-level information (§2, §6).
- **Global label shuffle (test).** XGB-rel trained on labels permuted across the whole training set gives Task A AUPRC 0.339 ± 0.020 at prevalence 0.350, AUROC 0.499 ± 0.039, and Task B AUPRC 0.028 ± 0.002 at prevalence 0.024, AUROC 0.515 ± 0.026. The CNN trained the same way: 0.277 / 0.386 and 0.019 / 0.391. The CNN's AUROC sits well below 0.5 because a label-free model's output still varies from night to night, and on 8 test nights that alone moves a pooled AUROC by about ±0.1. This is the same mechanism as its between-night shortcut, and the context in which its real Task B AUROC of 0.516 should be read. Nothing in the pipeline learns test labels from anything but training labels.
- **No future information, by test.** Task B features are computed from the history slice only. `s2_split.py` replaces every sample at or after t with white noise for 30 random windows and asserts that the 47-feature vector is unchanged: 30/30 unchanged, and the live function matches the cached training table row by row. The night-so-far features are label-independent (identical after permuting all labels). The test split is loaded by one stage only, after every selection was fixed, and regenerating test predictions from the saved models reproduces the first files byte-for-byte.

## 2. What the pooled scores are made of: the within-night shuffle (test)

A shuffle that permutes labels *within* each night keeps each night's severity and destroys timing, so whatever survives it is "which night is this", not "which minute".

| task | model | within-night shuffle AUPRC / AUROC | real model AUPRC / AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | XGB-rel (3 seeds) | 0.541 ± 0.012 / 0.729 ± 0.008 | 0.621 ± 0.015 / 0.811 ± 0.002 |
| A | CNN (1 seed) | 0.503 / 0.621 | 0.537 ± 0.012 / 0.651 ± 0.026 (3 seeds) |
| B | XGB-rel (3 seeds) | 0.058 ± 0.007 / 0.679 ± 0.009 | 0.109 ± 0.005 / 0.706 ± 0.006 |
| B | CNN (1 seed, matched) | 0.035 / 0.546 | 0.039 / 0.501 |

For Task A the CNN's real score is almost entirely night level (0.503 → 0.537). The feature model adds 0.08 AUPRC and 0.08 AUROC of genuine within-night ranking, which its within-night AUROC of 0.86 measures directly. For Task B, most of the pooled AUROC (0.679 of 0.706) is "which nights have onsets at all", and the timing-blind shuffle alone reaches lift 2.4 (0.058 / 0.024), which matters for the bar in §7. Compared seed-for-seed with its own shuffle, the Task B CNN is *below* its timing-blind control (AUROC 0.501 vs 0.546): on Task B it has nothing above night level. The timing-relevant number for XGB-rel is its within-night AUROC, 0.691 (record-bootstrap CI 0.564–0.842 over the 5 test nights with ≥ 5 onsets).

## 3. Prediction is hard in a specific way: horizon, history and the gap sweep

**Horizon sweep** (XGB-rel, 3 seeds, test, ongoing-at-t excluded). All horizons share one fixed set of prediction times valid for H = 10, which drops each night's last nine minutes, so the H = 1 row differs from the headline 0.109. AUROC is the prevalence-free column. Lift is bounded above by 1/prevalence, which falls from 41 at H = 1 to 6 at H = 10, so part of the fall in lift is mechanical.

| H (min) | prevalence | AUPRC | lift | AUROC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.024 | 0.088 ± 0.005 | 3.62 ± 0.19 | 0.705 ± 0.008 |
| 2 | 0.046 | 0.090 ± 0.008 | 1.93 ± 0.18 | 0.619 ± 0.007 |
| 3 | 0.065 | 0.083 ± 0.004 | 1.28 ± 0.06 | 0.587 ± 0.006 |
| 5 | 0.097 | 0.120 ± 0.009 | 1.23 ± 0.09 | 0.559 ± 0.020 |
| 10 | 0.162 | 0.220 ± 0.013 | 1.36 ± 0.08 | 0.611 ± 0.021 |

AUROC falls from 0.705 at one minute to 0.56–0.62 beyond two, and is not monotone (0.559 at H = 5, 0.611 at H = 10). A pure "apnea-prone state" signal would keep its ranking skill as the horizon grows, because onsets in the next ten minutes are also more likely in active periods; this one does not. The competing explanation is **label-grid smear**: the event has already begun physiologically in the last history minute, which the coarse minute grid still labels N, so the one-minute skill would be detection of an onset under way rather than prediction.

**History sweep** (window-only XGBoost, H = 1, test, common prediction times with ≥ 10 min of night elapsed, so W = 5 differs slightly from the headline): W = 1 min lift 3.80 ± 0.28, AUROC 0.770; W = 3: 4.42 ± 0.83, 0.736; W = 5: 5.50 ± 0.39, 0.738; W = 10: 7.39 ± 0.55, 0.769. AUROC is flat across history length while lift rises. More past improves precision, not ranking.

**Gap sweep (post-hoc, validation; the control that locates the information).** Onset in (t, t+1] predicted from a 5-minute history that *ends at t − g*; window-only XGBoost, 3 seeds, one common set of prediction times; within-night AUROC over the 3 validation nights with ≥ 5 onsets (a13, a14, a15), no CI:

| gap g (min) | prevalence | AUPRC | lift | AUROC | within-night AUROC (3 nights) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0.029 | 0.103 ± 0.014 | 3.54 | 0.709 ± 0.011 | 0.648 |
| 1 | 0.029 | 0.061 ± 0.004 | 2.08 | 0.636 ± 0.008 | 0.543 |
| 2 | 0.029 | 0.054 ± 0.005 | 1.87 | 0.622 ± 0.004 | 0.523 |

Removing the last 60 seconds of history removes about two thirds of the within-night skill (0.648 → 0.543 above 0.5, on 3 nights), about half of the lift (3.54 → 2.08 above 1) and a third of the pooled AUROC (0.709 → 0.636 above 0.5). Removing another minute changes little more. Most of the one-minute skill is therefore in the final minute before t. At minute-label resolution this cannot separate an onset already under way (which the grid still labels N) from a short precursor; the apnea-like signature the detector finds in that minute (§4, AUROC 0.658) and the +1 tilt of the label-shift test (§1) lean toward the former. What remains with a one-minute gap is small: lift about 2, within-night AUROC 0.54 on 3 validation nights. The same sweep with the round-2 Task B final family, logistic regression C = 0.5, on the same features and prediction times (`outputs/posthoc_gap_sweep_lr.md`), says the same thing more sharply: g = 0 AUROC 0.795, within-night 0.694, lift 3.07; g = 1 AUROC 0.641, within-night 0.508, lift 1.99; g = 2 AUROC 0.675, within-night 0.534, lift 2.18 (within-night on 3 validation nights, no CI; g = 2 above g = 1, 0.534 vs 0.508, is within noise on 3 nights, not a rebound). With the last minute removed, within-night ranking is at chance for the final model family. A sub-minute gap sweep (cutting the last 15 / 30 / 45 s) would separate the two readings and is listed under "more time".

## 4. Detection model evaluated on prediction windows, and the future-minute detector

- **Detector on the last history minute** (pre-registered, test). The Task A window-feature XGBoost, with its inputs computed causally from the history slice, scores the minute [t−1, t), labelled N in the primary set, against the onset label: AUPRC 0.035 ± 0.000 (lift 1.41), AUROC 0.658 ± 0.000; the Task A CNN, 0.029 / 0.452. A model trained for detection sees a faint apnea-like signature in the last normal minute. The Task B models score higher (AUPRC 0.109–0.121, AUROC 0.71–0.74), and the history sweep suggests why. On the history sweep's window set (≥ 10 min of night elapsed), a model *trained on the onset target* from one history minute reaches AUROC 0.770, while the detector on the last history minute reaches 0.658 over all primary windows. The sets differ by the first ten minutes of each night, so the comparison is indicative, but it points at the target rather than the length of the trajectory as what makes the difference.
- **Future-minute detector** (post-hoc, validation). The Task A XGB-rel applied to the horizon minute itself and scored on the onset label: AUPRC 0.041 ± 0.000, AUROC 0.625 ± 0.007, within-night AUROC 0.688 (validation prevalence 0.029; the window-only XGBoost 0.039 / 0.617 / 0.607). In the primary set "horizon minute is A" is identical to "onset", so this detector is scored on its own target with the future in hand. Matched model for model, it does not beat the legitimate predictor. The window-only version scores 0.039 pooled and 0.607 within nights against the gap-0 predictor's 0.103 and 0.648; the XGB-rel version scores 0.041 pooled against the selected model's validation 0.118 (mean of 3 seeds), and within nights 0.688 against the selected model's 0.698 (a13 0.723, a14 0.729, a15 0.642). The first minute of an event carries only a weak signature at minute resolution, and the predictor's advantage comes from the apnea-prone state in the history and the night so far.
- **Three formulations of Task B** (XGB-rel / CNN, test). Primary, ongoing-at-t excluded (prevalence 0.024): AUPRC 0.109 ± 0.005 / 0.039, AUROC 0.706 / 0.501. Deployment view, all windows, positive only on a new onset (prevalence 0.016): 0.056 ± 0.007 / 0.046, AUROC 0.704 / 0.701; the CNN recovers to the feature model's AUROC here because ongoing-event windows are easy negatives that make the task partly between-state again. Persistence-inflated, positive if apnea is anywhere in the horizon (prevalence 0.353): 0.615 ± 0.003 / 0.549, AUROC 0.824 / 0.660. The last is the shortcut a careless setup rewards: because events persist (mean run 21.7 min), a detector of the current minute looks like a predictor. Excluding ongoing windows removes that shortcut by construction, which is why the honest task is hard.

## 6. Which windows the prediction model gets right, and whether there is a pattern

The round-2 final (logistic regression, threshold 0.732) flags 284 of 2,452 test windows and catches 19 of 60 onsets (a16 9 of 13, a19 6 of 15, a20 4 of 16, a18 0 of 6, b05 0 of 7, c09 and c10 0 of 3), with 265 false alarms (a16 63, c08 56, b05 43); per-night AUROC a19 0.830, a16 0.664, a20 0.626, a18 0.524, b05 0.508. For the round-1 model (XGB-rel, threshold 0.430, max-F1 on validation): it flags 37 of 2,452 test windows and catches 6 of the 60 onsets (a16 2 of 13, a18 1 of 6, a19 1 of 15, a20 2 of 16, b05 0 of 7, c09 and c10 0 of 3), with 31 false positives, 13 of them in the control night c08. The ranking is more informative than the operating point: per-night AUROC a19 0.944, a20 0.774, a16 0.649, b05 0.645, a18 0.443 (one night below chance).

**Pattern (post-hoc stratification of the fixed test predictions, s7).** For the round-2 final (logistic regression), the pattern is quality and night, not recency: by minutes since the previous apnea minute the AUROC runs 0.605 / 0.397 / 0.625 / 0.762 / 0.640 (≤ 1, 2–3, 4–10, 11–30, > 30 min or none; the 2–3 min stratum below chance); by SQI tercile 0.614 / 0.775 / 0.843 with TPR 0.24 / 0.25 / 0.53; flat or clipped windows (243, 10 %) hold 99 of its 265 false alarms (FPR 0.419 vs 0.077); catches concentrate on a16 (9 of 19) and two eligible nights sit at chance (a18 0.524, b05 0.508). For the round-1 model (XGBoost with night-so-far features) the pattern was recency instead: onsets within 30 minutes of a previous event ranked at AUROC 0.69–0.86 against 0.601 after a long quiet stretch (strata of 6–16 onsets, SE ≈ 0.07–0.10, the > 30 min stratum pooling the control nights), and 5 of its 6 catches followed an event within 30 minutes. Middle and last thirds of the night rank better than the first for both models.

For the round-1 model, quality: worst / mid / best SQI tercile AUROC 0.752 / 0.708 / 0.661, TPR 2/25, 3/20, 1/15, too few events to be informative. The worst tercile holds more onsets (25 vs 15). AUROC(SQI → label) uses the SQI as the score and label 1 as the positive, so a value above 0.5 means the positive minutes are cleaner: on train it is 0.435 for Task A (apnea minutes noisier) and 0.544 for Task B (onset windows slightly cleaner), which disagrees with the test terciles; neither effect is large. Either way quality and label are mildly entangled, in opposite directions on train and test and both small; that is the circularity the quality clause was written to avoid rather than evidence that noise drives the errors. For the round-1 model, error excess is not concentrated in flat or clipped minutes (243 such windows, 7 onsets, AUROC 0.913).

**The deep model (post-hoc diagnostics, validation only, not in the results table).** Selecting the CNN by within-night AUROC instead of pooled AUPRC gives validation within-night AUROC 0.655 ± 0.010 for Task A (pooled-selected: 0.579) and 0.590 ± 0.004 for Task B; random polarity flips during training give 0.635 ± 0.045 and 0.502 ± 0.036. Neither cheap remedy closes the gap to the feature models' within-night ranking (0.79 on validation). We attribute the between-night shortcut (REPORT §4–6) to 16 training nights but did not test that; a learning curve is listed under "more time".

## 7. The pre-registered bar, and the verdict

The stamped text and its amendments, with timestamps, are in `PREREGISTRATION.md`: the bar (11:46), amendments 1–3 (11:48, 11:58, 12:08, all before any Task B test number), and amendment 3a (12:12, after test metrics were first displayed at 12:08:46; it fixed the tie threshold and the median-seed rule, the latter already in code at 12:08:00). The bar: *"short-horizon prediction is learnable here if the H = 1 onset model beat prevalence AUPRC by ≥ 2× with CIs clear of prevalence across seeds, and errors concentrated away from signal-quality failures"*, with (i) within-night AUROC > 0.5 with a record-bootstrap CI clear of 0.5 over a/b test+validation nights with ≥ 5 onsets, and (ii) true-positive rate in the worst SQI tercile not lower than in the best, evaluated on the single model selected on validation.

**Result.** The stamped bar is met on its literal reading. Point lift 4.2–4.6× on all three seeds, with every CI lower bound (0.030–0.038) above prevalence (0.024); within-night AUROC 0.694 (CI 0.599–0.781) over the eight eligible nights, 0.691 test-only (CI 0.564–0.842 by the results bootstrap; a second draw in `outputs/prereg_bar.md` gives 0.550–0.842); TPR in the worst SQI tercile not lower than in the best (2/25 vs 1/15 onsets, too few to be informative; the frozen threshold detects 6 of 60 test onsets). It is not met on the stricter reading, in which the CI lower bound must clear twice prevalence (0.030–0.038 vs 0.048). Note what carries it. The "≥ 2× prevalence" clause alone is passed by the timing-blind within-night shuffle (lift 2.4, §2), so clause (i), the within-night AUROC with its CI clear of 0.5, is the part of the bar that measures timing. That is why amendment 1 exists.

**What we conclude.** The result that would have convinced us was written down before any Task B test number existed: the bar above. We observed it on its literal reading and not on its stricter one. The bar tests above-chance within-night ranking at a one-minute horizon, and that is met. It does not test lead time, and the post-hoc controls show the lead time is mostly confined to the final minute. When the history stops one minute earlier, about two thirds of the within-night skill on the 3 eligible validation nights, half of the lift and a third of the pooled AUROC are lost (gap sweep), and the skill does not survive longer horizons (horizon sweep). At minute-label resolution the round-1 Task B model (and, at the same within-night level, the round-2 logistic regression) is best described as detecting, in the final minute before t, either an onset already under way or its immediate precursor, plus a small night-level prior; the detector's apnea-like signature in that minute and the +1 label-shift tilt lean toward the former. Having seen these controls, we would now also require, stated as post-hoc and not as the bar, within-night AUROC clear of 0.5 with the history cut off one minute before t, and a horizon-2 AUROC not far below horizon-1. The first is not established (0.543 on 3 nights, no CI) and the second is not observed (0.619 vs 0.705). "The numbers came out lower" is not the explanation; the controls say *where* the information is, in the final minute before the prediction time.
